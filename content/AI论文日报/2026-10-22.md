### [LongZero: Enhancing Long-Context Reasoning in LLMs from Zero Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30132v1)

- **原标题**：LongZero: Enhancing Long-Context Reasoning in LLMs from Zero Data
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-10-21T17:59:14+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Zhennan Wan、Jianfei Chen · cs.CL、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Reinforcement learning \(RL\) is an effective way to strengthen the long-context reasoning of large language models \(LLMs\), but it needs training data that are long, diverse, challenging, and verifiable. Human annotation of such data is expensive, naturally occurring data are too scarce to sustain RL, and existing synthesis methods depend on external corpora or stronger teacher models, which become harder to obtain as the trained models get stronger. Recently, RL from zero data, in which a model learns from tasks it proposes itself, has achieved excellent performance, but existing methods target short-context tasks. Therefore, we propose $\\textbf{LongZero}$, a method that lets an LLM improve its own long-context reasoning without any external data. Instead of writing questions and answers over existing documents, LongZero first has the model synthesize the question and answer with its supporting evidence in a compact structured form, and only then separately expands the evidence into a long multi-document context. LongZero combines these self-written multi-hop reasoning tasks with self-written multi-round retrieval tasks and trains the model with GRPO. We evaluate LongZero on two LLMs across five long-context reasoning benchmarks and compare it with RL on data synthesized from external corpora. Experimental results show that LongZero can generalize to real tasks, and outperforms both baselines, suggesting a viable and promising path to improving long-context reasoning of LLMs.

### [Think Harder Where It Matters: Item-Level Adaptive Recursion for Sequential Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30128v1)

- **原标题**：Think Harder Where It Matters: Item-Level Adaptive Recursion for Sequential Recommendation
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-10-21T17:58:06+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Zhanshuo Cao、Jiakai Tang、Yuqi Zhou、Sunhao Dai、Jun Xu · cs.IR、cs.AI、cs.CL、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Existing sequential recommenders usually allocate a fixed computational budget to every item in a user's interaction history, regardless of the item's contribution to the final prediction. We challenge this de facto design with two diagnostic findings. \(1\) Non-uniform computational need: items within the same sequence vary drastically in how much computation they require. Most converge after shallow processing while a critical minority only stabilizes at full depth. \(2\) Suboptimality of fixed allocation: given oracle access to the target item, a non-uniform depth assignment consistently outranks the fixed one, indicating that existing uniform computation both wastes capacity on already-converged items and sacrifices overall recommendation quality. These observations motivate a shift from fixed-depth computation to item-level adaptive reasoning. To this end, we propose LoopRec. LoopRec recursively applies a single weight-shared block and introduces a lightweight gate at each recursion boundary that makes a binary exit-or-continue decision for every item independently. Items whose representations have sufficiently matured exit early, while those requiring deeper reasoning continue to subsequent recursions. This design converts the fixed global depth into a learned, per-item property without auxiliary depth labels, and is readily adaptable to diverse backbone architectures. Extensive experiments on public benchmarks with four backbone families demonstrate that LoopRec consistently outperforms matched fixed-depth counterparts. Further analysis reveals that LoopRec's learned allocation exhibits interpretable patterns aligned with our post-hoc oracle analysis: lower-frequency items and more recent interactions naturally receive deeper computation. Our code is available at https://github.com/Elpmis117/LoopRec.

### [PhysiCalWorld: In-Context Physics Calibration for World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30125v1)

- **原标题**：PhysiCalWorld: In-Context Physics Calibration for World Models
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-10-21T17:57:59+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Yunhao Luo、Wentao He、Yinpei Dai、Hyelin Nam、Hongze Fu、Joyce Chai、Nima Fazeli、Jeong Joon Park · cs.RO、cs.AI、cs.CV
- **来源摘要**：Existing world models typically perceive a scene through current visual frame or a narrow history window. However, these observations may not reveal the physics properties that determine transition dynamics, such as an object's mass distribution, joint damping, or surface friction. Without calibrating these physics factors for world models, predictions tend to regress toward an empirical prior and induce severe drift in novel test scenes. To this end, we propose PhysiCalWorld, which calibrates action-conditioned world models to the instance-specific dynamics via in-context prompting, without fine-tuning or analytical physics modeling. Our method encodes an exploratory interaction clip collected within the test scene into a compact physics descriptor token, which grounds subsequent predictions in the scene's actual physical behavior. PhysiCalWorld is trained end-to-end with the latent prediction loss alone, without physics labels. At inference time, the descriptor token is extracted once and can be reused across rollouts with negligible compute overhead. We evaluate PhysiCalWorld across four environments covering diverse physics variations and show that it substantially improves rollout prediction accuracy and visual planning success rate. The learned descriptors also form a physically structured latent space and generalize beyond the physics ranges seen during training.

### [Self-normalized Bernstein Inequalities for Regenerative Processes and Applications to Learning Without Independence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30119v1)

- **原标题**：Self-normalized Bernstein Inequalities for Regenerative Processes and Applications to Learning Without Independence
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-10-21T17:56:37+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Imon Banerjee、Pierre Alquier · cs.LG、math.PR、math.ST
- **来源摘要**：We prove a self-normalized Bernstein inequality for bounded functions of a regenerative process with independent, identically distributed marked cycles of finite mean length. The bound uses observed cycle lengths and retains explicit terms for truncation and the cycle ending after the observation horizon. Selecting a tuning parameter from a finite deterministic collection gives a version of Bernstein inequality where all terms are empirical but one, related to the tails of the regeneration times. We then study this term in many regimes: it turns out that it is of second-order in many cases of interest. This is important for applications in machine learning: we prove generalization error bounds for classification with non-i.i.d., regenerative data. These bounds can be used to define learning objectives: by minimizing the first-order term only in the bound, we define agnostic classifiers that still satisfy oracle inequalities. We also show that the bound on the average deviation provided by our results is minimax-optimal in the sample size and in the expected regeneration time. Finally, we combine the Bernstein inequality with a regenerative strong approximation to obtain a Gaussian approximation using an observable block variance estimator.

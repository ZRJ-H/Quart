### [Why Do LLMs Help with Scientific Misconduct Requests and What to Do About It?: Increasing the Trustworthiness of AI for Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25492v1)

- **原标题**：Why Do LLMs Help with Scientific Misconduct Requests and What to Do About It?: Increasing the Trustworthiness of AI for Science
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-19T04:32:25+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Almene De Meran Meguimtsop、Daniel E. Acuna · cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：Large language models \(LLMs\) support scientific research, but some of that assistance can also undermine research integrity. Existing safety evaluations in science often measure whether models refuse misconduct requests, but rarely explain whether they even recognize the problem or why they comply. We investigate this gap across 8 LLMs using SciIntBench, an existing benchmark of 1,350 prompts about scientific research integrity. We examine the reasons behind compliance in four steps. First, when asked directly whether fulfilling a request would involve misconduct, models overwhelmingly identify such requests, even in the Most Covert cases \(91%\), while they refuse to carry out the request in only 34% of cases. Second, we examine which request characteristics are associated with refusal using features that capture how the request is framed, how much the assistant has been told about what it is helping to hide, and how the goal and the user's stake are stated, among others. Third, we estimate that the most significant effect was to rephrase requests as questions about permissibility rather than instructions, which increased refusal from 52% to 88%. Finally, we test two system-prompt fixes: asking models to assess the request before responding and reminding them of responsible conduct of research principles. These changes increase refusal rates on adversarial requests from 58% to 87% and 86%, respectively. Our findings show that models recognize wrongful requests but still help users violate integrity. Understanding why these failures occur and testing ways to reduce them offer promising paths toward more trustworthy AI for science.

- **原标题**：When Should a Voice Agent Speak? End-of-Turn Detection in Turkish
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-19T04:26:13+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Ahmet Tuğrul Bayrak、Fatma Nur Korkmaz、Mustafa Sertaç Türkel、Nur Yağmur Tuncer · cs.AI、cs.CL
- **来源摘要**：A voice agent that waits for a fixed silence before replying either interrupts users who pause mid-utterance or responds late to users who have finished. This study examines the decision at the onset of silence, using turn ends and turn-internal pauses extracted from the Real-TurnTurk corpus of Turkish dyadic video calls. A fine-tuned Turkish TurnGPT model, a zero-shot Turkish language model and a deployed multilingual detector are compared, together with voice-activity timing and facial features, under recording-disjoint and speaker-disjoint evaluation with paired cluster bootstrap tests. On held-out recordings, the fine-tuned model separates turn ends from pauses better than both text baselines and is the best calibrated of the three. Timing features of the current speaker are similarly informative, and their combination with the fine-tuned text score gives the best timing-based results; with logistic regression, this combination also outperforms the same combination with either text baseline. In a latency simulation, adding the text score to a silence timeout shortens the mean response delay at the same interruption rate. On automatic Whisper transcripts, the fine-tuned model retains its advantage over the zero-shot model.

### [Planning Width Is Hard to Approximate Even Given the Unique Optimal Plan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25484v1)

- **原标题**：Planning Width Is Hard to Approximate Even Given the Unique Optimal Plan
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-19T04:19:23+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Viet Bac Nguyen · cs.DS、cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：For each fixed $k$, iterative-width search finds optimal plans in polynomial time on tasks of bounded tuple-chain width, but the approximability of planning width has remained open even when an optimal plan is supplied. When all optimal plans visit the same state sequence, we characterize set and chain width as hitting numbers of competing states. We construct positive-precondition STRIPS tasks with a supplied unique optimal plan, a single reachable goal state, binary branching, and constant-size actions. Every reachable state lies on a goal-reaching execution at most one action longer than optimal, yet distinguishing width $1$ from $Θ\(N\)$, where $N$ is the number of atoms, is NP-hard for set, chain, and goal-admissible width. Unless $\\mathrm{P}=\\mathrm{NP}$, no polynomial-time algorithm achieves a one-sided approximation factor $o\(N\)$ or a symmetric factor $o\(\\sqrt{N}\)$, and simple estimates attain both orders. For positive-precondition tasks with a supplied unique optimal plan of length at least two, testing set or chain width against a threshold $k\\geq 1$ is coNP-complete for each fixed $k$ and $Σ\_2^{\\mathrm{P}}$-complete when $k$ is part of the input. Both results also hold for goal-admissible width if only one goal state is reachable. On an explicit state graph, a greedy algorithm approximates set width within a logarithmic factor even when optimal plans branch. Thus, knowing an optimal plan does not eliminate the difficulty of certifying substantially smaller width.

### [WeightCode: Installing New Capabilities into Neural Networks via Weight-Space Programming](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25476v1)

- **原标题**：WeightCode: Installing New Capabilities into Neural Networks via Weight-Space Programming
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-19T04:04:29+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Bo Li、Taoyong Cui、Bob Zhang、Mengran Li、Chengyang Zhang、Yang Zhang、Pheng Ann Heng、Wanli Ouyang、Zhenfei Yin、Yingcheng Wu、Ling Yang · cs.LG、cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：Neural networks encode learned capabilities in their weights, yet extending those capabilities with explicit task knowledge remains a fundamental challenge. Here we show that Weight-Space Programming \(WSP\), in which executable programs read, construct or modify a network's weights directly, can install new capabilities within trained neural networks. WeightCode, a WSP framework, uses a frozen language model to write executable weight programs that install new computations into trained neural networks using task knowledge and their existing weights, without gradient-based training. Once installed, the new capabilities run independently of the language model. Given a grammar or eight demonstrations, weight programs enable previously unseen command compositions \(0% to 99.8% exact match\) and a new pickup action \(0% to 98.4% success\), while preserving the original command and navigation capabilities. On ImageNet, four weight programs achieve net gains of 8.84-17.62 points while preserving clean accuracy, outperforming the best of eight fine-tuning methods under matched examples, checks, and retention constraints. In a Vision Transformer, causal interventions show that installed noise-correction gates drive robustness gains, while their selective activation preserves clean accuracy. Rerun on five independently trained Vision Transformers without further language model calls, ten weight programs retain 83-110% of their source-network gains on average, whereas copying their fixed weight edits yields negligible gains. Six programs written for an already edited network add 5.23-14.50 points of contrast robustness while retaining the previously installed noise gain. Together, these results establish WSP as a new route to acquiring and accumulating capabilities in trained neural networks through reusable weight programs.

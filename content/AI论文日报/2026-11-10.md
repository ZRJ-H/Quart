### [EmbodiedIntentBench：面向移动机器人任务的意图驱动运行时适配基准](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10498v1)

- **作者/分类**：Rajat Bhattacharjya、Changwoon Lee、Hyeonjong Ju、Sing-Yao Wu、Hamidreza Alikhani、Zhuoyi Yang、Ian Harris、Nalini Venkatasubramanian、Nikil Dutt · cs.RO、cs.AR、cs.CV、cs.DC、cs.LG
- **核心摘要**：论文提出EmbodiedIntentBench，用于评估移动机器人任务中意图驱动的运行时适配。该基准用结构化契约表示任务意图，涵盖覆盖、时序证据、报告、紧迫性和资源，并把模拟无人机工作负载与因果、资源可核算的执行耦合，使计算决策影响任务时间、资源使用以及哪些观测能贡献证据。作者在多阶段任务以及变化的紧迫性、连通性和电池条件下评估固定与自适应感知策略。
- **研究问题**：移动机器人运行时策略的评估需要判断其决策是否达成任务目标，而现有评估多聚焦孤立感知精度或执行效率，缺少在物理运动与资源约束下衡量任务完成度的基准。
- **方法**：EmbodiedIntentBench用覆盖、时序证据、报告、紧迫性和资源的结构化契约表示任务意图，将模拟无人机工作负载与因果、资源可核算的执行耦合，并在多阶段任务及变化的紧迫性、连通性和电池条件下比较固定与自适应感知策略。
- **实验结果**：多阶段Depot研究中，87.5%全库目标召回率的配置仅完成16个任务中的1个，较低召回率配置完成14个；忽略执行导致的观测损失会提高总体成功率并反转两种策略排序；Jetson AGX Orin CPU和GPU回放改变本地与远程策略的相对成功率；选定GPU回放中意图驱动适配在成功率不变下最多降低28%建模任务能耗。

- **价值点**：提供了在物理与资源约束下按任务完成度评估运行时决策的可扩展框架，并显示忽略执行引起的观测损失会误导策略比较。

### [Safer for Whom? A Systematic Exploration of AI Safety in Eating Disorder Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10488v1)

- **原标题**：Safer for Whom? A Systematic Exploration of AI Safety in Eating Disorder Interactions
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-07T05:30:37+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Jinyuan Wang、Minh Duc Chu、Frances Ross、Ellen Fitzsimmons-Craft、Kristina Lerman · cs.HC、cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：AI assistants advise users about diet, exercise, and weight loss---areas in which unsafe guidance can worsen body dissatisfaction, dietary restriction, compulsive exercise, and other behaviors associated with eating disorders \(EDs\). Existing evaluations emphasize diagnostic accuracy, overtly harmful prompts, or synthetic scenarios, providing limited insight into whether models recognize implicit vulnerability in natural interactions or protect users consistently. We evaluate responses from three open-weight large language models to 100 questions sourced from an online pro-anorexia community. We grade responses using clinically informed criteria that extend a seven-dimensional taxonomy of ED-related harms. We also construct matched conditions in which questions are accompanied by information about the user varying race, gender, body mass index \(BMI\), and explicit ED vulnerability. Safety varies substantially across models and harm dimensions. High-risk responses are approximately twice as frequent without bios, suggesting that models do not consistently recognize vulnerability from language alone. Explicit signals of ED vulnerability improve safety by 10--14 points across models. Responses also differ significantly by race, gender, and BMI, with body size producing the largest disparities. These patterns are consistent with reliance on the stereotype that EDs primarily affect thin, White women, potentially leaving users outside this profile with less protective responses.

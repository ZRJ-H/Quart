### [AmbiSteer: Selectively Eliciting Ambiguity-Aware Responses in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17900v1)

- **原标题**：AmbiSteer: Selectively Eliciting Ambiguity-Aware Responses in Vision-Language Models
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T08:40:07+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Monica Riedler、Samuele Antonelli、Barbara Plank、Raffaella Bernardi · cs.CV、cs.CL
- **来源摘要**：Vision-language models \(VLMs\) often exhibit overconfidence, providing firm answers even when visual evidence is insufficient. A prominent case is referential ambiguity in Visual Question Answering \(VQA\), where multiple entities in an image are plausible referents of a question. Addressing such cases requires recognizing when ambiguity is present: ambiguous questions can be handled by asking for clarification or enumerating plausible referents, while unambiguous questions should receive a direct answer. Current VLMs struggle with this distinction, often defaulting to a single interpretation. To investigate whether activation steering can serve as an inference-time mechanism to elicit such behaviors, we introduce AmbiSteer, a lightweight framework to selectively elicit ambiguity-aware behavior. Across three VLMs, we find that steering alone has limited and model-dependent effects, while prompting is generally more effective. Combining prompting and steering substantially increases clarification and enumeration behaviors, but also elicits them on unambiguous cases. AmbiSteer addresses this using a linear classifier to predict input ambiguity and condition the steering direction accordingly, increasing the proportion of inputs handled appropriately from 59.0% to 77.0% on average across models. Our work sets the ground for lightweight approaches to reduce model overconfidence in uncertain multimodal settings.

### [More Genes, Faster Inference: Efficient Large-Panel Spatial Transcriptomics Prediction from Histology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17899v1)

- **原标题**：More Genes, Faster Inference: Efficient Large-Panel Spatial Transcriptomics Prediction from Histology
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T08:35:27+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Songming Zhang、Yuxiao Luo、Shifu Luo、Siran Chen、Shuquan Su、Jinyan Li · cs.CV、cs.AI、q-bio.QM
- **来源摘要**：Spatial transcriptomics \(ST\) prediction from histology aims to infer spatially resolved gene expression profiles directly from tissue images. This is a challenging problem that is currently often addressed with complicated model architectures, additional inputs, and complicated training procedures, while remaining limited to fixed, small gene panels. This raises a natural question: Is there an efficient method for large-panel ST prediction? Here, we present GeneFlow, a simpler yet effective framework for large-scale spatial gene expression prediction that unifies flow-matching generative modeling with structured multimodal fusion. Specifically, GeneFlow first encodes both histological morphology and cross-gene interactions in spatial spots through histology and gene tokenizers. The model directly predicts spatial gene expression, rather than an intermediate velocity field, while being trained with a velocity-based flow-matching objective. We show that, even without relying on heavy generative pipelines, GeneFlow achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple ST benchmarks, with up to 56.8% gains in 1,000-gene prediction. Extensive experiments further show that GeneFlow produces biologically meaningful spot profiles while reducing computational cost by orders of magnitude, thereby improving efficiency and scalability, particularly in clinical settings. We believe GeneFlow provides an opportunity for leveraging widely available histology images for downstream biological discovery. Code is available at: https://github.com/ZZhangsm/GeneFlow

### [Hierarchical Bayesian physics-augmented neural networks for populations of hyperelastic laws: specimen variability and uncertainty from scarce, noisy data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17892v1)

- **原标题**：Hierarchical Bayesian physics-augmented neural networks for populations of hyperelastic laws: specimen variability and uncertainty from scarce, noisy data
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T08:29:29+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Vincent C. Scholz、Phaedon-Stelios Koutsourelakis · cs.CE、cs.LG、stat.ML
- **来源摘要**：The hyperelastic law of a material such as soft tissue differs between specimens, and often only a few specimens can be tested. Physics-augmented neural networks \(PANNs\) learn such laws from data without a prescribed functional form while keeping them physically admissible, and Bayesian versions of related models quantify the uncertainty of the law of one specimen or of a pooled group of specimens. A pooled fit, however, attributes the differences between specimens to measurement noise or to uncertainty about a mean law, and neither describes a new specimen. We first develop a Bayesian PANN for one specimen, whose priors keep the learned law plausible for few, noisy data. We then extend it to a hierarchical Bayesian PANN, a nonlinear mixed-effects model in which all specimens share learned polyconvex features and each specimen is a draw from a population of laws. Features, specimens, and population are inferred jointly by variational inference, with a separate model of the measurement noise, so that the variability between specimens is not mistaken for noise, the population carries its own uncertainty, and every specimen drawn from it is polyconvex. The population predicts the response of new specimens with credible intervals, without data or from a partial test, and assigns them to cohorts with quantified uncertainty, all without a prescribed form of the law. On synthetic populations, it recovers the population from few specimens and predicts new specimens more reliably than three published methods. On Treloar's rubber and on healthy and aneurysmal aortas, it reaches the accuracy of a deterministic PANN and assigns no aorta specimen to the wrong cohort with confidence, although it underestimates the uncertainty of untested curves.

### [Quantum Graph Neural Networks: Architectures, Trainability, and Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17886v1)

- **原标题**：Quantum Graph Neural Networks: Architectures, Trainability, and Applications
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T08:23:31+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Zeinab Sharifi、Hossein Shirazi · cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Quantum graph neural networks \(QGNNs\) apply parametrized quantum circuits to graph-structured data, with the aim of overcoming limits of classical graph neural networks in scalability, expressivity, and long-range information flow. Since the first Hamiltonian-based model in 2019, the field has produced many architectures, applications, and claims, but the evidence behind them has not been assessed systematically. This survey introduces a unified formalism that describes every QGNN as an encoder, a graph-conditioned circuit, observables, and an optional classical head, together with a circuit-level condition for permutation equivariance and a resource model for comparing costs. We organize the literature into five architecture families \(convolutional, equivariant, message-passing and recurrent, Hamiltonian, and hybrid\) and compare them by qubit cost, symmetry, scalability, and evidence. We then review symmetry and geometric inductive biases \(permutation, rotation, and Lorentz\), the results on expressivity, trainability, noise, and scalability, and applications in high-energy physics, chemistry, finance, healthcare, earth observation, and transportation. An audit of datasets and evaluation practices leads to a minimum reporting protocol. Our main finding is that the design space is rich and symmetry is the best-supported inductive bias, whereas the evidence remains thin: results are mostly simulated, on small graphs, at parity with classical models or with fewer parameters, and no advantage has yet been established on tasks over classical graph data. We identify five open challenges and a research agenda covering provable advantage, equivariant and geometric designs, fault-tolerant implementations, and shared benchmarks. The coverage extends through 2026 and emphasizes recent work.

### [Securing Multi-Agent Systems by Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17883v1)

- **原标题**：Securing Multi-Agent Systems by Design
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T08:22:06+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Xilong Wang、Xiaoxing Chen、Neil Gong · cs.CR、cs.AI、cs.CL
- **来源摘要**：Multi-agent systems solve complex tasks by decomposing them into subtasks, delegating the subtasks to agents, and aggregating the agents' responses using an aggregation rule. Malicious agents can manipulate the final solution by providing malicious responses to their assigned subtasks. In this paper, we first propose response manipulation attacks \(ReMA\), which iteratively optimize malicious responses to maximize attack effectiveness. Specifically, we formulate untargeted and targeted attacks as optimization problems and develop an approach to approximately solve these problems by iteratively refining malicious responses based on feedback from a surrogate multi-agent system. To defend against such attacks, we further propose SecMAS, a secure aggregation rule that leverages the semantic similarities among agent responses to select the response that is most consistent with a sufficiently large subset of the other responses. We theoretically establish security guarantees for SecMAS against both untargeted and targeted attacks. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that existing aggregation rules are vulnerable to ReMA, whereas SecMAS effectively defends against all evaluated attacks, including adaptive variants of ReMA, while maintaining utility comparable to that of existing aggregation rules.

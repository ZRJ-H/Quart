### [Pairing AIS with RF: Analysis of a Markovian Approach for Maritime Data Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18563v1)

- **原标题**：Pairing AIS with RF: Analysis of a Markovian Approach for Maritime Data Fusion
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T18:59:43+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Daniel Grimm、Kyra Jongman、Renato Cordeiro Ferreira、Giuseppe Cascavilla、Alfredo Cuzzocrea、Willem-Jan Van Den Heuvel · cs.LG、cs.CR
- **来源摘要**：Monitoring maritime activities is important for safety and security, particularly for detecting illegal activities, but remains challenging due to the size and complexity of the maritime domain. The Automatic Identification System \(AIS\) is commonly used for this purpose, despite its known vulnerabilities. In contrast, space-based radio frequency \(RF\) observations provide an independent source of vessel activity but lack ship identification and therefore cannot identify vessels on their own. Following the CRISP-DM process, this study applies a Pair Hidden Markov Model \(PHMM\) to align individual RF detections with AIS trajectories, using temporal and spatial information while modeling noise and timing discrepancies. We evaluate it on NOAA AIS data with synthetic RF detections against nearest-neighbor baselines and analyze its errors with bias tests on trajectory length, sampling density, stationarity and AIS--RF distance. The length-corrected Forward score reaches a top-1 precision of $0.7776$ on the validation split of multi-candidate detections, where errors are possible, and an AUC of $0.637$ for detecting dark vessels among the detections that pass the prefilter. With a single RF detection, the score mostly reflects the AIS trajectory itself, and its errors favor longer and more stationary trajectories. A log-odds score, which compares the full model with a null model without match transitions, raises precision to $0.8580$ and AUC to $0.852$. Its length-corrected variant no longer shows a significant bias towards stationary trajectories and short inter-message distances, but still favors candidates farther from the RF detection. A time-weighted nearest-neighbor baseline without fitting remains ahead \(precision $0.9013$, AUC $0.904$\), so this single-detection, hand-designed PHMM formulation approaches but does not beat it.

### [FETTUCCINE: Fast and efficient brain-to-text decoding on mobile devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18561v1)

- **原标题**：FETTUCCINE: Fast and efficient brain-to-text decoding on mobile devices
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T18:59:02+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Jonathan D. McCart、Pranav I. Deevi、Mehdi Azabou、Nanda H. Krishna、Caleb A. McKinney、Nicholas S. Card、Sergey D. Stavisky、Chethan Pandarinath · q-bio.NC、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Recent advances in speech neuroprostheses have enabled new communication avenues for people who have lost the ability to speak due to neurological illness or injury. These systems can decode neural activity during attempted speech into text by relying on language priors to achieve high decoding accuracy. This performance currently comes at the cost of using a compute-intensive language modeling stack, presenting a major bottleneck for real-world use. Further, current language models incur highly variable and disruptive inference latencies, limiting communication throughput and preventing users from reliably participating in natural conversation. We introduce FETTUCCINE, an end-to-end brain-to-text framework that leverages the speed and portability of automatic speech recognition \(ASR\) models to address the aforementioned practical challenges, while achieving usable \(&lt;10%\) word error rates \(WER\), efficient adaptation to neural non-stationarities, and reliable performance on mobile devices. When evaluated using publicly available data from an intracortical speech neuroprosthesis user, FETTUCCINE outperforms previous end-to-end methods, achieving as low as 4.39% WER. Most notably, our models successfully run on a commercially-available mobile device with throughput reaching up to 370x real-time, providing the first demonstration of brain-to-text decoding on a mobile device. We also show that our models can be successfully finetuned to extend usable performance to future days. Beyond performance, our approach enables localization of the neural features most relevant for decoding via gradient-based saliency maps, which we show align with well-established physiological priors across different ASR model families. Taken together, our results show that FETTUCCINE overcomes core infrastructural and computational barriers, yielding a new class of portable brain-to-text communication.

### [Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562v1)

- **原标题**：Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T18:59:02+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Jiaming Tang、Zhuoyang Zhang、Junxian Guo、Fangzhou Zhao、Yilong Zhao、Zekai Li、Xingyang Li、Yuyang Zhao、Song Bian、Zhijian Liu、Song Han · cs.RO、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Robot-use agents \(RUAs\) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent \(CUA\), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics \(PEAR\) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning \(RURL\), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $π\_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $π\_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

### [BAL: Bidirectional Autoregression in Latent Space for Learning Human Movement Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18559v1)

- **原标题**：BAL: Bidirectional Autoregression in Latent Space for Learning Human Movement Representations
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T18:58:54+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Genki Kinoshita、Ko Nishino · cs.CV、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Learning semantically meaningful human movement representations from 3D pose sequences is essential for human behavior modeling. Existing self-supervised methods derive supervision from artificial assumptions, e.g., invariance to augmentation within a certain range or recoverability of masked regions at a certain temporal scale, introducing heuristic hyperparameters that make the learned semantics sensitive to their settings. We propose BAL, a self-supervised framework that instead exploits supervision intrinsic to human movement: the natural ordering structure of everyday behavior. BAL autoregressively predicts in a semantically abstract latent space, analogous to how humans anticipate others' actions rather than their precise joint configurations. Bidirectional autoregressive prediction, both forward and backward in time, fully leverages these ordering constraints while enabling representation extraction using both past and future context at inference. To suppress representation collapse, BAL decomposes each prediction target into a global component capturing sequence-level semantics and a local component capturing context-independent movement. We prove that the local targets retain nontrivial angular diversity unless the representations are completely collapsed, preserving a meaningful training signal. Experiments on large-scale datasets demonstrate that BAL's representations are highly effective for motion captioning, forecasting, and interpolation, outperforming baselines on most evaluation metrics.

### [WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18553v1)

- **原标题**：WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-13T18:58:08+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Aayush Marasini、Barsat Khadka、Nick Rahimi · cs.LG、cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：Recent work shows that large language models \(LLMs\) can operate directly on the parameters of other neural networks. They can read what a network was trained to do, fix a network that fails, and write working networks from scratch, all without gradient descent. This capability has been termed Weight-Space Programming \(WSP\). Exploring this new paradigm systematically requires a robust benchmark. We introduce WEIGHTBENCH, a benchmark that evaluates LLMs of increasing capability on 42 WSP tasks across five tracks: reading, fixing and writing weights, recognizing edits that are impossible, and data-free compression. The networks whose weights the LLMs read and edit range from 2K-parameter control policies to 7B-parameter language models. Experimental benchmark results show that frontier agents can already read small networks and write working control policies without ever running them, but cannot yet patch language models, compress them without data, or tell an impossible edit from a hard one. On all 42 tasks, Claude Opus 5.5 reaches a macro score of 0.535 \(Sonnet 5.5 0.466, Haiku 5.5 0.402, DeepSeek v4-flash 0.215\); on the 38 tasks it was run on, GPT-6.1-sol reaches 0.568 against Opus's 0.546. Code, the task registry, our OS-level sandbox harness, and the live leaderboard are publicly available at https://github.com/Aayush-Marasini/WeightBench.

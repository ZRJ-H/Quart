### [Optimal Linear Regression Without a Variance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27983v1)

- **原标题**：Optimal Linear Regression Without a Variance
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-20T18:58:45+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Artin Tajdini、Jasper C. H. Lee、Jonathan Scarlett、Kevin Jamieson · stat.ML、cs.AI、cs.IT、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：We study high-probability linear regression in the infinite variance regime, wherecthe regression noise has only a finite conditional $α$-moment $ν$ for some unknown $α\\in\(1,2\)$, and the covariates may only have finite moments up to a given order \(e.g., 4th moment\). We give a black-box reduction that converts finite-variance regression procedures into weak-moment procedures. The reduction is based on empirical residual pruning and is agnostic to the moment exponent, the noise scale, the covariance, and the design moment. Under a finite fourth-moment condition on the covariates, any finite-variance subroutine attaining the sub-Gaussian rate yields the following when the number of samples $n$ is above a suitably defined threshold: Without knowing $α$ or $ν$, we have with probability at least $1-δ$ that $ \\|\\widehatθ-θ^\*\\|\_Σ \\lesssim\_α ν^{1/α} \\big\(\\frac{d+\\log\(1/δ\)}{n}\\big\)^{\\frac{α-1}α}, $ where $d$ is the dimension, $Σ$ is the covariate covariance matrix, and $\\|v\\|\_Σ:=\(v^\\topΣv\)^{1/2}$. We further prove a matching minimax lower bound that holds under the same conditions. The reduction is computationally efficient whenever the finite-variance subroutine is. We also obtain a distribution-free prediction guarantee under a bounded raw $α$-moment assumption on the response, and give an analogous $α$-agnostic reduction for mean estimation.

- **原标题**：A Methodologically Rigorous Benchmark of Linear Regression,RNN, and LSTM for S&amp;P 500 Forecasting:Identifying Sources of Performance Inflation in Financial DeepLearning
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-20T18:57:15+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Hari Sitaula、Joseph Opitz · cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：This study examines one-day-ahead forecasting of the S&amp;P 500 closing level using a 2006-2025 dataset that includes fundamental price data, macroeconomic indicators, and engineered technical features. We benchmark three model families, namely linear regression, vanilla recurrent neural networks \(RNN\), and long short-term memory networks \(LSTM\), within a unified preprocessing and chronological evaluation pipeline. A central contribution of this work is a methodological audit of common practices in the deep-learning-for-stock-prediction literature. We identify three sources of result inflation that frequently appear in published methodologies: wavelet-denoised evaluation targets, the absence of a naive persistence baseline, and best-of-N replicate reporting. We then re-evaluate all model families under a corrected protocol that controls for each. We compare models on accuracy, stability across replicates, and architectural depth sensitivity, using the naive persistence forecast \(predicting today's close as tomorrow's close\) as a primary baseline against which all candidate models must demonstrate improvement.

### [MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27973v1)

- **原标题**：MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-20T18:51:29+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Chang Nie、Chaoyou Fu、Songlin Liu、Yiqing Hu、Caifeng Shan · cs.CL
- **来源摘要**：Deploying large language models \(LLMs\) in long-horizon and continual interactions requires maintaining reusable memory states for future inference, posing challenges for existing architectures. Standard Transformers accumulate layer-wise, token-level KV caches, incurring linear GPU memory growth and quadratic computational complexity as the context expands. In contrast, linear-time architectures compress history into fixed-size states, but struggle to retain fine-grained details. We introduce MemOnce, a foundation architecture that learns scalable memory states for language models. MemOnce is based on two core designs: \(a\) Memory encoder-decoder: it caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory representations and dynamically decodes them back into KV caches for attention layers; \(b\) Memory sparse attention: for each continual input, MemOnce adaptively retrieves relevant memory representations, with the sparse indices computed only once across layers. Coupling these two designs enables efficient on-the-fly memory loading and KV cache decoding, drastically reducing the GPU memory footprint and achieving linear-time complexity. We conduct extensive experiments to show that MemOnce outperforms Transformers and other architectures across varying model sizes, context lengths, and training paradigms \(pretraining and post-training adaptation\). In particular, adapting Qwen3.5-4B with MemOnce reduces memory state cache by 1313$\\times$ and prefill latency by 6.9$\\times$ at 1M context, and improves throughput by 3.5$\\times$ and overall memory performance by +5.77%. Finally, MemOnce generalizes zero-shot to downstream representation tasks, achieving competitive results against specialized models.

### [Non-penetrating World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27963v1)

- **原标题**：Non-penetrating World Models
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-20T18:44:57+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Yuhang Dai、Yinwei Wu、Ziyue Lin、Han Ji、Xingyi Yang · cs.CV、cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers \(DiTs\) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights.

### [Fine-Grained Japanese Clinical Named Entity Recognition: A Controlled Comparison of BERT and Open-Weight Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22219v1)

- **原标题**：Fine-Grained Japanese Clinical Named Entity Recognition: A Controlled Comparison of BERT and Open-Weight Large Language Models
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-17T05:01:24+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Feng Han、Ayako Yagahara、Kenji Hirata、Atsushi Yoshida、Kohsuke Kudo · cs.AI、cs.CL
- **来源摘要**：Background: Generative large language models \(LLMs\) offer an alternative to encoder-based clinical named entity recognition \(NER\), but their performance on fine-grained Japanese extraction tasks and sensitivity to generation settings remain uncertain. Methods: We compared two fine-tuned BERT-CRF baselines with nine open-weight LLMs on iCorpus, which defines 113 entity categories. The evaluation used 433 sentences from 36 test documents; 1,672 sentences were used for training. LLMs were evaluated using zero-shot prompting with and without JSON Schema constraints and three-seed QLoRA fine-tuning. Fine-tuned models were assessed under different output-token limits, including 900 and larger model-specific limits of 2,600 or 4,096 tokens. Performance was measured using sentence-level label-surface matching with relaxed and exact-surface F1. Results: The highest zero-shot relaxed F1 was 0.337, compared with 0.836-0.841 for the BERT baselines. At the larger output limits, all eight fine-tuned LLMs with at least 13B total parameters exceeded both BERT baselines \(relaxed F1, 0.882-0.914; exact-surface F1, 0.840-0.880\). Across three-seed means, increasing the output limit improved relaxed F1 by 0.052-0.085, alongside higher recall and fewer length-limited outputs. JSON Schema constraints changed relaxed F1 by -0.014 to +0.008 among seven models outside the two reasoning-oriented configurations, while medical-general model pairs showed no consistent advantage for medical specialization. Conclusions: On this historical Japanese case-report benchmark, task-specific fine-tuning combined with adequate generation capacity was associated with stronger LLM extraction performance. Output-token limits materially affected comparisons with BERT, whereas enforcing output structure alone did not overcome low zero-shot accuracy.

### [Learning Optimal Transport Plans Via Autoregressive Token Regression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22218v1)

- **原标题**：Learning Optimal Transport Plans Via Autoregressive Token Regression
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-17T04:57:26+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Ivan Jaen Marquez、Sotirios Panagiotis Chytas、Vikas Singh · cs.LG、cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：Optimal Transport \(OT\) provides a principled framework for finding the minimum cost coupling between probability distributions. Classical solvers remain the gold standard for this task. At the same time, OT is an ideal testbed for studying whether learning-based amortized models can internalize the structure of OT type problems. Here, ground-truth solutions are available and marginal constraints are explicitly defined. Also, exact discrete solutions have well-characterized sparsity. We describe ToR-OT, a transformer-based model that learns to predict discrete OT plans through autoregressive token regression. We reframe OT into a structured sequence generation task. ToR-OT decodes sparse transport plans as sequences instead of solving an optimization task at test time. We focus on three properties: \(a\) Zero-shot generalization: pretraining on a large synthetic corpus of OT instances allows a single model to generalize to unseen distribution pairs and heterogeneous cost metrics without retraining, \(b\) Sparse primal decoding: by exploiting the inherent sparsity in exact discrete OT solutions, the decoder predicts only nonzero entries \(avoids dense coupling outputs\), \(c\) Amortized inference: on low-dimensional discrete problems, ToR-OT gives accurate approximate plans and compares favorably with per-instance neural OT baselines. Overall, ToR-OT is not yet a replacement for classical solvers, but a step toward understanding how autoregressive transformers can learn structure of constrained optimization problems like OT.

### [Communicate the Change: Efficient Expert Parallelism via Activation Compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22215v1)

- **原标题**：Communicate the Change: Efficient Expert Parallelism via Activation Compression
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-17T04:52:41+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Fengyuan Liu、Jay Gala、Siva Reddy、Dzmitry Bahdanau · cs.LG、cs.AI、cs.DC
- **来源摘要**：Scaling has driven the success of large language models \(LLM\), with sparse mixture-of-experts \(MoE\) architectures enabling greater parameter capacity without a proportional increase in computation. At large scales, MoE models rely on expert parallelism to distribute experts across devices. Each MoE layer requires two synchronous collective communication steps during inference: dispatching hidden states to the activated experts and combining the resulting expert outputs. As compute throughput has outpaced interconnect bandwidth, these synchronous collectives have become a major bottleneck to MoE scaling and efficiency. We address this challenge with EchoMoE, a lightweight architecture modification that retrofits existing MoE models, requiring approximately 8,500x fewer training tokens than full pretraining. Motivated by the observation that transformer blocks update the residual stream incrementally, we use a hidden state from an earlier layer as a reference and communicate only the compressed difference between it and the current state, rather than communicating each hidden state in full. EchoMoE periodically refreshes these reference states and transmits only the compressed differences synchronously, reducing the traffic on latency-critical paths without significant computational overhead. EchoMoE reduces inter-node communication volume by 58% and improves throughput by 22.6%, while preserving average performance within 3.8 percentage points across 18 benchmarks. EchoMoE further improves inference throughput by up to 2.38x over bandwidth-constrained Ethernet, paving the way for wide-area distributed inference. EchoMoE also tolerates quantization of the communicated representations without measurable quality loss. We release an efficient implementation for applying EchoMoE to pretrained MoE models, together with an inference integration for vLLM.

### [Sample-efficient Personalization of Large Language Model Alignment with Multidimensional User Preference Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22210v1)

- **原标题**：Sample-efficient Personalization of Large Language Model Alignment with Multidimensional User Preference Data
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-17T04:47:45+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Zhen-Yu Zhang、Jerry Huang、Gang Niu、Takashi Ishida、Masashi Sugiyama · cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Preference alignment has become a central tool to align large language models \(LLMs\) with human values and desired behavior, and its online variant can further advance their capabilities beyond what static data alone offer by repeatedly generating and labeling new responses with human feedback. But costly, time-consuming annotations limit its scalability, making sample efficiency essential, and existing methods typically seek to improve it by expanding coverage of the response space or selecting pairs with the highest uncertainty for comparison. Although they are effective, these methods quantify uncertainty from the perspective of the LLM, which may differ from the uncertainty perceived by the user: a pair can appear highly uncertain to the LLM even when past comparisons from the user already imply a clear ordering. In this paper, we propose multidimensional preference-guided online direct preference optimization \(MPG-DPO\), which leverages low-cost multidimensional evaluations from automated verifiers on the user historical comparisons to construct a personalized preference cone guiding response selection for online preference alignment. Specifically, it first discards responses that are confidently dominated under the estimated user preference, then selects two responses that are competitive under the retained preference cone yet exhibit the strongest unresolved disagreement. Theoretical analysis shows that, with high probability, the fraction of queries spent on cone-dominated responses vanishes as learning progresses. Experiments across multiple LLM families show that MPG-DPO outperforms strong contenders under matched query budgets.

- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-17T04:41:45+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Sooyeon Song、Jea-Hyun Park · math.DS、cs.LG、nlin.AO
- **来源摘要**：This study proposes a Cucker-Smale model incorporating dynamic networks and the discrete $p$-Laplacian \($p \\in \(1,2\)$\) and analyzes the relationship between flocking time constraints and graph connectivity from both theoretical and experimental perspectives. We establish that this model achieves flocking within finite time and derive a quantitative relationship between the lower bound of flocking time and graph volume. Based on these results, we observe that imposing sufficiently small flocking time induces an increase in network volume, and that larger initial velocity variance requires greater volume under the same time constraint. In our experiments, we trained a transformer-based PPO policy to select network structures at each time step. Under the most stringent time constraint, the maximum average number of connections increased sharply, confirming a threshold effect. The negative correlation between convergence time and average number of connections reflects the theoretical results well. This study provides quantitative evidence for the phenomenon that time constraints induce connection mobilization.

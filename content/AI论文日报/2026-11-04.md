### [DreamX：把视频扩散模型用作通用潜在动作学习器](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05377v1)

- **原标题**：DreamX: Video Diffusion Model is a Universal Latent Action Learner
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-03T18:59:56+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Jisoo Kim、Chaehyun Kim、Junsu Kim、Jiho Park、Seongchan Kim、Eunbeen Hong、Soowon Son、Fu-En Yang、Seungryong Kim · cs.CV、cs.AI、cs.RO
- **核心摘要**：DreamX 将预训练视频扩散模型改造为通用潜在动作学习器，从视频模型丰富的时空特征中提取紧凑的潜在动作，把每个潜在视频帧从 1200 个 token 压缩到 4 个 token，压缩约 300 倍。作者称，借助视频模型的生成先验，这些潜在动作能捕捉与动作相关的动态，并与视觉外观解耦，从而形成跨人类与机器人、跨环境和跨动作参数化的共享动作接口。在 LARY-Bench 上，DreamX 在潜在动作模型中取得最优表现；在一个设定中编码的动作在另一设定中仍然有效，支持任意到任意的视频迁移，以及跨多种具身和环境的零样本动作迁移。该共享接口还支持在紧凑潜在动作空间中进行策略学习，降低基于视频扩散的策略的计算成本。
- **研究问题**：如何从视频中学习一种通用动作表示：同一动作的不同执行之间保持一致，同时对具身和外观变化保持不变。
- **方法**：复用预训练视频扩散模型，从其时空特征中提取紧凑潜在动作，每帧压缩到 4 个 token；利用生成先验使潜在动作与视觉外观解耦。
- **实验结果**：在 LARY-Bench 上取得潜在动作模型中的最优表现；支持任意到任意的视频迁移和跨具身、跨环境的零样本动作迁移；在紧凑潜在动作空间中做策略学习可降低基于视频扩散的策略的计算成本。
- **工程价值**：潜在动作空间可作为跨具身共享接口，并降低视频扩散策略的计算开销。
- **价值点**：该工作把视频生成模型的先验用于动作表示学习，同时给出压缩比例、基准排名和零样本迁移三类可核对的结果。

### [PolicyIK: High-Precision Inverse Kinematics for Robot Arms with Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05372v1)

- **原标题**：PolicyIK: High-Precision Inverse Kinematics for Robot Arms with Reinforcement Learning
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-03T18:59:14+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Yuhan Liu、Xinqi Liu、Zixuan Dong、Keith W. Ross、Shenji Wan · cs.RO、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Inverse kinematics \(IK\) converts end-effector pose commands into joint motion, and many vision-language-action \(VLA\) policies rely on it to act on a given arm and to transfer across embodiments. Precise IK remains laborious: redundancy and singularities complicate the inverse mapping, execution depends on hand-tuned controllers, and training data on physical robots are costly to collect. We present PolicyIK, a goal-conditioned reinforcement learning policy for high-precision IK. It updates joint-position references from pose feedback and observes its accumulated reference relative to the measured joints, so it can retain the offset that the position controller needs at rest. Two-scale pose errors with an error-dependent action gain resolve millimeter residuals while preserving large motions. Progress and dwell rewards teach it to both reach the tolerance and stay within it. In simulation, a single attempt by the trained PPO policy keeps the mean errors over the final second within 1 mm and 0.2° on 93.5% of held-out tasks, and a deployment protocol raises success to 98.9%. When trained directly on a physical arm with an off-policy RL learner, PolicyIK achieves higher reaching and holding success than all four classical IK controllers under the same time budgets. In simulated pick-and-place, PolicyIK executes the end-effector commands of the $π\_{0.5}$ VLA policy with 95.7% success. These results show that reinforcement learning can provide high-precision inverse kinematics for robot arms. Code is available at https://github.com/Tinaliu0123/PolicyIK.

### [GUILD: Guided Iterative Data Collection for Imitation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05368v1)

- **原标题**：GUILD: Guided Iterative Data Collection for Imitation Learning
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-03T18:57:35+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Shivin Dass、Kevin Nguyen、Rohan Yelandur、Junhong Xu、Roberto Martín-Martín · cs.RO、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Imitation learning policies in robotics are trained on datasets collected through costly real-world teleoperation. Often, the data-collection process is unguided, relying on the teleoperator's intuition, resulting in data that contributes little to overall policy performance. Recent work has shown that curating such unguided datasets can substantially improve policy performance, but these approaches operate by filtering existing datasets after collection and therefore do not prevent the acquisition of uninformative data. In this work, we propose GUILD, a novel Guided Data Collection \(GDC\) method that actively reasons about which data should be collected to maximize downstream policy performance. We introduce an iterative framework that alternates between 1\) task proposal and data collection and 2\) data evaluation to close the loop. For task proposal, we leverage semantic priors from vision-language models to identify relevant environments, object configurations, and goals for data collection. However, since semantic relevance does not imply policy improvement, we perform data evaluation. By leveraging predictive data attribution we approximate how the collected trajectories influence policy performance on a target deployment setting. This signal is then used as feedback to guide subsequent data collection, closing the data collection loop. We evaluate our approach in simulation and real robot settings, demonstrating that guided data collection substantially improves sample efficiency and deployment performance compared to passive data collection strategies, multiple baselines and experienced roboticists. More information at https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/guild2026/

### [Evidence-grounded large language models guide perovskite solar cell design and optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00633v1)

- **原标题**：Evidence-grounded large language models guide perovskite solar cell design and optimization
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-10-31T04:57:32+00:00
- **作者/分类**：YanKai Chen、Zhi Wan、ZiHao Li、Tao Jing · cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：Using large language models to interpret experimental evidence, design discriminating experiments and formulate well-supported mechanistic explanations remains a central challenge in materials research. Here we establish PSM-Bench, an 800-question benchmark developed by perovskite researchers to evaluate scientific reasoning in perovskite photovoltaics. We also introduce OpenPerov, an expert-informed language-model framework that learns from expert-calibrated evidence records reconstructed from 4,532 articles; these records link experimental strategies, controls and observations to mechanistic interpretations. Structured-evidence adaptation outperformed conventional question-answer training, and OpenPerov Flash ranked first among 13 closed-book systems. OpenPerov Pro raised Scientific Mastery further, from 87.6 to 89.1, by revising selected passages with retrieved evidence. On questions derived from subsequent studies, both models also outscored expert-authored answers. In an experimental test of self-assembled monolayer design, OpenPerov selected a K analogue that outperformed the Na analogue preferred by experts. Devices incorporating the selected contact reached a power conversion efficiency of 27.05% and retained 96.74% of their steady-state reference efficiency after 1,000 h of dark thermal ageing at 85 $^\\circ$C. These results show that reconstructed scientific reasoning provides an effective basis for building domain models that interpret experiments and inform materials design.

### [TopoQuant: Topological Framework to Characterize Behavioral Shifts in Quantized Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00631v1)

- **原标题**：TopoQuant: Topological Framework to Characterize Behavioral Shifts in Quantized Large Language Models
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-10-31T04:55:14+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Baha Rababah、Shahzeb Qamar、Lorenz Sparrenberg、Rafet Sifa、Murat Kantarcioglu、Carson K. Leung、Cuneyt Gurcan Akcora · cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：Post-training quantization \(PTQ\) reduces the com putational cost of large language models \(LLMs\), but it can introduce internal structural and behavioral changes that are not captured by traditional metrics such as accuracy and per plexity. We present TopoQuant, a topological framework that uses topological data analysis \(TDA\) to quantify structural drift in self-attention weights and relate it to behavioral changes in quantized LLMs. Across four LLM families and multiple quantization levels, we show that topological drift remains stable at moderate compression levels but increases sharply below 4 bit precision, closely tracking declines in attention agreement. Our results reveal a strong relationship between topological drift and attention agreement, indicating that structural changes in model parameters closely reflect behavioral degradation under quantization. We further demonstrate that topological features can accurately predict attention consistency using lightweight machine learning models, achieving R2 scores of up to 0.85. Predicting attention agreement enables efficient assessment of be havioral consistency in quantized LLMs, allowing quantization induced degradation to be detected without running expensive downstream tasks. The results indicate that topological drift pro vides an efficient early-warning signal for quantization-induced degradation and can help identify safe compression limits without extensive downstream evaluation.

- **作者/分类**：Yusuke Hashimoto、Takaaki Tomai · cs.AI、cond-mat.mtrl-sci、cs.MA
- **来源摘要**：Extracting knowledge from large databases of experimental materials data requires repeated analyses and reusable records of their choices and results. We developed Multi-Agentic AI \(MAA\), a multi-agent system in which AI agents with distinct roles analyze experimental thermoelectric data under predefined instructions and quality checks, saving analysis records traceable to their sources. In four experiments varying theme and cycle counts, we measured what accumulated in these records. In the largest experiment \(100 analyses across 10 themes and 10 cycles\), concept co-occurrences were concentrated within themes, vocabulary was shared across themes and some relation descriptions were first detected in later cycles. A specialized AI that searches this experiment's knowledge bases and reads their analysis records answered materials questions with cited sources. Retrospective checks, including tracing an answer generated with a preliminary corpus back to the source data, exposed errors: traceability makes errors findable but not absent.

- **来源摘要**：Accelerating modern foundational models has pushed domain-specific compilers toward increasingly complex, multi-tiered optimization pipelines. ### [Log-Driven Semantic Tuning for Mixed Integer Programming Solvers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00621v1)

- **原标题**：Log-Driven Semantic Tuning for Mixed Integer Programming Solvers
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-10-31T04:43:28+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Zhengzhong Ricky You、Kaizhao Sun、Zeping Min、Wotao Yin、Xinshang Wang · math.OC、cs.AI、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：Automated configuration of mixed-integer programming \(MIP\) solvers largely relies on black-box optimization. Each trial requires an expensive solver run and provides only a scalar outcome, so tuning can require many such evaluations. Human experts take a different approach. They inspect solver logs, identify performance bottlenecks, and adjust the parameters that address them. We propose Log-Driven Semantic Tuning \(LDST\), which uses a large language model \(LLM\) to automate this process. LDST extracts compact diagnostics from solver logs, uses the LLM to propose sparse edits to the current best configuration based on parameter meanings and observed solver behavior, and validates each proposal before execution. A regime-adaptive objective selects the tuning signal according to the information available from a time-limited run, while a log-based completion signal estimates completion time from branch-and-bound tree growth recorded in ordinary solver logs, so that solved and unsolved runs share one time scale. On 215 instances from five benchmark sets, LDST reduces geometric-mean loss by 43.9% on easy instances and 16.7% on intermediate instances relative to the best of SMAC3, RBFOpt, and random search. In both regimes, LDST achieves a lower loss with 10 proposals than any of these baselines achieves with 50. Configurations that LDST tunes on a single instance also transfer better than baseline configurations to unseen instances of the same family.

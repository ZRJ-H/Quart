### [Jailbreak Detection on Large Language Models via Contrastive Prototype Structures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24936v1)

- **原标题**：Jailbreak Detection on Large Language Models via Contrastive Prototype Structures
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-18T18:56:12+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Yan Qiu、Yuanyuan Li · cs.CL
- **来源摘要**：Jailbreak attempts and harmful prompts remain a challenge for the safe deployment of large language models. We study whether contrastively learned representations can support safety detection without training an additional downstream classifier. We propose Jailbreak Detection with Prototype Inference \(JDPI\), which uses binary safety labels to construct harmful and benign prototypes and makes predictions from relative prototype distances. JDPI uses only binary safety labels for encoder adaptation, prototype construction, and validation. In our setup, it uses fewer labeled examples than the classifier baseline \(Method A\) by avoiding the separate split used to train the downstream classifier. Across four benchmarks covering different input formats, contrastive fine-tuning improves JDPI's detection accuracy by 9.1-18.0 percentage points. Using the same encoder, JDPI achieves slightly higher ROC-AUC on one benchmark and stays within 0.017 of the baseline across all four. With dataset-specific adaptation, JDPI exceeds the released Llama Guard 3-8B baseline by 0.173-0.310 in ROC-AUC across the four benchmarks. In multi-turn replay on TomGibbs, JDPI uses accumulated user history as the conversation unfolds, achieving higher conversation-level accuracy than when it uses the current turn alone. Across 12 directed transfers, JDPI outperforms the classifier by an average of 1.59 percentage points in accuracy, demonstrating stronger cross-dataset robustness.

### [Knowing What You Know: A Trait Theory of Reviewer Value in Human-AI Cognitive Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24932v1)

- **原标题**：Knowing What You Know: A Trait Theory of Reviewer Value in Human-AI Cognitive Systems
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-18T18:55:07+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Priyanka Sinha、Sugam Pandey · cs.AI、cs.CE、cs.HC、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：When a capable model and a human reviewer cooperate on the same decision, which human still adds value? We argue this is a question about a single cognitive trait, metacognitive calibration: the alignment between a reviewer's behavioral confidence and the actual correctness of their judgments. We develop a trait theory of reviewer value to make it precise. The theory makes five claims: value is governed by calibration rather than by activity, speed, agreement, or tenure; calibration leaves observable traces in behavioral telemetry; those traces are non-diagnostic on their own, because the fast, low-revision signature of a calibrated expert is indistinguishable from that of a confident novice until it is gated by outcomes; human calibration and a model's self-knowledge are the same construct, so the joint system should defer to whichever agent is better calibrated on each item; and individual calibration is causally identifiable only above the level of the single judgment. We ground each claim in a six-month production window of an AI-assisted accounting system \(541621 reviewer events, 483 reviewers\), and find that a purely behavioral expertise profile inverts the ground truth, scoring an overconfident cohort \(5.2 percent correction survival\) higher than genuine experts \(99.2 percent\); that reviewer quality is flat across tenure, so the trait is not learned on the job; and that the same calibration logic that separates good from bad reviewers, applied to the model, concentrates 92.5 percent of human effort on genuine errors. We close with the boundary the theory must respect: under majority-vote aggregation, individual causal value is unidentifiable.

### [Agentic Harness Engineering Beta: Requirement-Centered Evolution of Agent Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24924v1)

- **原标题**：Agentic Harness Engineering Beta: Requirement-Centered Evolution of Agent Harnesses
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-18T18:52:13+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Jiahang Lin、Jiahao Chen、Yiding Wang、Shichun Liu、Chengjun Pan、Shihan Dou、Honglin Guo、Yuyang Hu、Changhao Jiang、Zhenhua Han、Hang Yan、Qi Zhang、Tao Gui、Xuanjing Huang · cs.SE、cs.AI
- **来源摘要**：Real-world harness engineering is driven by explicit and implicit user requirements, which guide development and validation. However, existing harness evolution methods often overlook implicit requirements and interaction needs, while using a single evolution agent to modify the harness and benchmark scores to select changes. This process often produces narrow patches and leads to benchmark-specific local optima. We introduce Agentic Harness Engineering Beta \(AHE-Beta\), a requirement-centered loop that brings real-world engineering practices into harness evolution. The loop consists of three steps: \(1\) User-driven evaluation lets simulated users raise their requirements as issues while they work with the harness over multiple turns. \(2\) Team-based modification implements those requirements as generalizable changes rather than task-specific patches. \(3\) Requirement-driven selection merges a change only when it resolves a verified requirement and passes review. Empirically, on held-out tasks, AHE-Beta raises pass@1 from 21.9% to 33.3% while using 40.8% fewer tokens than Base Harness, and achieves higher pass@1 than Claude Code, Meta-Harness, and AHE. In a blind human A/B test, real users prefer AHE-Beta in 31 of 48 valid pairs and rate it higher on all five dimensions, matching the directional preference of the user simulator. The human A/B results show that AHE-Beta achieves its goal of aligning harness evolution with real-world user requirements. Without further evolution, the gains transfer to GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with pass@1 improvements of 15.8% and 5.3% over Base Harness. Together, these results show that requirement-centered harness evolution is a more general method for improving agent capabilities.

### [Decomposing SGD Dynamics in Neural Networks: Teacher-Induced Spikes and Variance Inflation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24908v1)

- **原标题**：Decomposing SGD Dynamics in Neural Networks: Teacher-Induced Spikes and Variance Inflation
- **来源与时间**：arXiv · 2026-11-18T18:43:40+00:00
- **作者/分类**：Samet Demir、Zafer Dogan · stat.ML、cs.LG
- **来源摘要**：We study two-layer neural networks trained by stochastic gradient descent \(SGD\) on a multi-index teacher model, focusing on how first-layer parameters orthogonal to the teacher subspace affect generalization. We decompose the first-layer weights into a signal component aligned with the teacher and an orthogonal complement, and analyze their joint SGD dynamics together with the second layer in the proportional limit. We show that although SGD updates to the complement are initially isotropic, the training dynamics induce teacher-dependent spectral spikes in the complement Gram matrix. This emergent anisotropy provides a concrete mechanism by which representation variance is amplified, and generalization degrades. To isolate this effect, we introduce Decomposed Dynamics, an analytically tractable surrogate whose asymptotic test-error dynamics coincide with those of SGD. In simulations with a ReLU student, freezing the directions of the orthogonal complement improves generalization relative to training them. Finally, we show that the signal component rapidly aligns with the second-layer weights, revealing strong cross-layer coupling even in this shallow setting. Together, our results provide a dynamical explanation for how feature learning outside the teacher subspace harms generalization.

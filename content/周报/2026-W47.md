  - [AmbiSteer: Selectively Eliciting Ambiguity-Aware Responses in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17900v1)
    - 摘要：Vision-language models \(VLMs\) often exhibit overconfidence, providing firm answers even when visual evidence is insufficient. A prominent case is referential ambiguity in Visual Question Answering \(VQA\), where multiple entities in an image are plausible referents of a question. Addressing such cases requires recognizing when ambiguity is present: ambiguous questions can be handled by asking for clarification or enumerating plausible referents, while unambiguous questions should receive a direct answer. Current VLMs struggle with this distinction, often defaulting to a single interpretation. To investigate whether activation steering can serve as an inference-time mechanism to elicit such behaviors, we introduce AmbiSteer, a lightweight framework to selectively elicit ambiguity-aware behavior. Across three VLMs, we find that steering alone has limited and model-dependent effects, while prompting is generally more effective. Combining prompting and steering substantially increases clarification and enumeration behaviors, but also elicits them on unambiguous cases. AmbiSteer addresses this using a linear classifier to predict input ambiguity and condition the steering direction accordingly, increasing the proportion of inputs handled appropriately from 59.0% to 77.0% on average across models. Our work sets the ground for lightweight approaches to reduce model overconfidence in uncertain multimodal settings.
  - [More Genes, Faster Inference: Efficient Large-Panel Spatial Transcriptomics Prediction from Histology](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17899v1)
    - 摘要：Spatial transcriptomics \(ST\) prediction from histology aims to infer spatially resolved gene expression profiles directly from tissue images. This is a challenging problem that is currently often addressed with complicated model architectures, additional inputs, and complicated training procedures, while remaining limited to fixed, small gene panels. This raises a natural question: Is there an efficient method for large-panel ST prediction? Here, we present GeneFlow, a simpler yet effective framework for large-scale spatial gene expression prediction that unifies flow-matching generative modeling with structured multimodal fusion. Specifically, GeneFlow first encodes both histological morphology and cross-gene interactions in spatial spots through histology and gene tokenizers. The model directly predicts spatial gene expression, rather than an intermediate velocity field, while being trained with a velocity-based flow-matching objective. We show that, even without relying on heavy generative pipelines, GeneFlow achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple ST benchmarks, with up to 56.8% gains in 1,000-gene prediction. Extensive experiments further show that GeneFlow produces biologically meaningful spot profiles while reducing computational cost by orders of magnitude, thereby improving efficiency and scalability, particularly in clinical settings. We believe GeneFlow provides an opportunity for leveraging widely available histology images for downstream biological discovery. Code is available at: https://github.com/ZZhangsm/GeneFlow
  - [Hierarchical Bayesian physics-augmented neural networks for populations of hyperelastic laws: specimen variability and uncertainty from scarce, noisy data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17892v1)
    - 摘要：The hyperelastic law of a material such as soft tissue differs between specimens, and often only a few specimens can be tested. Physics-augmented neural networks \(PANNs\) learn such laws from data without a prescribed functional form while keeping them physically admissible, and Bayesian versions of related models quantify the uncertainty of the law of one specimen or of a pooled group of specimens. A pooled fit, however, attributes the differences between specimens to measurement noise or to uncertainty about a mean law, and neither describes a new specimen. We first develop a Bayesian PANN for one specimen, whose priors keep the learned law plausible for few, noisy data. We then extend it to a hierarchical Bayesian PANN, a nonlinear mixed-effects model in which all specimens share learned polyconvex features and each specimen is a draw from a population of laws. Features, specimens, and population are inferred jointly by variational inference, with a separate model of the measurement noise, so that the variability between specimens is not mistaken for noise, the population carries its own uncertainty, and every specimen drawn from it is polyconvex. The population predicts the response of new specimens with credible intervals, without data or from a partial test, and assigns them to cohorts with quantified uncertainty, all without a prescribed form of the law. On synthetic populations, it recovers the population from few specimens and predicts new specimens more reliably than three published methods. On Treloar's rubber and on healthy and aneurysmal aortas, it reaches the accuracy of a deterministic PANN and assigns no aorta specimen to the wrong cohort with confidence, although it underestimates the uncertainty of untested curves.

  - [Pairing AIS with RF: Analysis of a Markovian Approach for Maritime Data Fusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18563v1)
    - 摘要：Monitoring maritime activities is important for safety and security, particularly for detecting illegal activities, but remains challenging due to the size and complexity of the maritime domain. The Automatic Identification System \(AIS\) is commonly used for this purpose, despite its known vulnerabilities. In contrast, space-based radio frequency \(RF\) observations provide an independent source of vessel activity but lack ship identification and therefore cannot identify vessels on their own. Following the CRISP-DM process, this study applies a Pair Hidden Markov Model \(PHMM\) to align individual RF detections with AIS trajectories, using temporal and spatial information while modeling noise and timing discrepancies. We evaluate it on NOAA AIS data with synthetic RF detections against nearest-neighbor baselines and analyze its errors with bias tests on trajectory length, sampling density, stationarity and AIS--RF distance. The length-corrected Forward score reaches a top-1 precision of $0.7776$ on the validation split of multi-candidate detections, where errors are possible, and an AUC of $0.637$ for detecting dark vessels among the detections that pass the prefilter. With a single RF detection, the score mostly reflects the AIS trajectory itself, and its errors favor longer and more stationary trajectories. A log-odds score, which compares the full model with a null model without match transitions, raises precision to $0.8580$ and AUC to $0.852$. Its length-corrected variant no longer shows a significant bias towards stationary trajectories and short inter-message distances, but still favors candidates farther from the RF detection. A time-weighted nearest-neighbor baseline without fitting remains ahead \(precision $0.9013$, AUC $0.904$\), so this single-detection, hand-designed PHMM formulation approaches but does not beat it.
  - [FETTUCCINE: Fast and efficient brain-to-text decoding on mobile devices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18561v1)
    - 摘要：Recent advances in speech neuroprostheses have enabled new communication avenues for people who have lost the ability to speak due to neurological illness or injury. These systems can decode neural activity during attempted speech into text by relying on language priors to achieve high decoding accuracy. This performance currently comes at the cost of using a compute-intensive language modeling stack, presenting a major bottleneck for real-world use. Further, current language models incur highly variable and disruptive inference latencies, limiting communication throughput and preventing users from reliably participating in natural conversation. We introduce FETTUCCINE, an end-to-end brain-to-text framework that leverages the speed and portability of automatic speech recognition \(ASR\) models to address the aforementioned practical challenges, while achieving usable \(&lt;10%\) word error rates \(WER\), efficient adaptation to neural non-stationarities, and reliable performance on mobile devices. When evaluated using publicly available data from an intracortical speech neuroprosthesis user, FETTUCCINE outperforms previous end-to-end methods, achieving as low as 4.39% WER. Most notably, our models successfully run on a commercially-available mobile device with throughput reaching up to 370x real-time, providing the first demonstration of brain-to-text decoding on a mobile device. We also show that our models can be successfully finetuned to extend usable performance to future days. Beyond performance, our approach enables localization of the neural features most relevant for decoding via gradient-based saliency maps, which we show align with well-established physiological priors across different ASR model families. Taken together, our results show that FETTUCCINE overcomes core infrastructural and computational barriers, yielding a new class of portable brain-to-text communication.
  - [Training Reasoning Models in Simulation to Natively Use Real Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18562v1)
    - 摘要：Robot-use agents \(RUAs\) have emerged as a promising approach to general-purpose robot control. However, strong performance has so far been achieved only by frontier proprietary models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.2, whereas open-weight models remain substantially less capable. We show that an open-weight 27B reasoning model can be trained to use real robots natively through reinforcement learning, entirely in simulation and without demonstrations. Like a computer-use agent \(CUA\), the model observes the environment, reasons in a chain of thought, and generates the next end-effector pose, without any harness. We build a Post-training Engine for Agentic Robotics \(PEAR\) and use it to train the model on simulated robot tasks at scale with robot-use reinforcement learning \(RURL\), mirroring how frontier models acquire computer use. We find that the skills acquired in simulation generalize well beyond the training tasks: they transfer to an unseen robot, to the real world, and to novel instructions. After training on LIBERO and RoboDojo in simulation, the model succeeds in 60% of our trials on a real dual-arm robot, whereas the base model succeeds in none. On LIBERO, training raises the success rate from 1.6% to 74.4%, approaching GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol at maximum reasoning effort. Compared with $π\_{0.5}$, a vision-language-action model fine-tuned on demonstrations, our model is substantially more robust to changes in the task itself. On LIBERO-Pro, $π\_{0.5}$ succeeds in only 0.75% of episodes when assigned a new task in a familiar scene whereas our model succeeds in 57.4%. Finally, the model retains its general capabilities, with no measurable degradation on nine reasoning, coding and vision benchmarks. These results highlight robot-use reinforcement learning as a promising component of foundation-model post-training.

  - [Latent Lattice Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21608v1)
    - 摘要：Language models typically increase per-token computation through deeper or looped layers and latent reasoning steps, while richer residual connections improve information flow across layers. We instead increase computational capacity: the number of active computational states a model maintains. We introduce the Lattice of Latents, a set of per-layer horizontal latent streams that run recurrently along time, alongside the vertical residual stream that flows through depth. Like hyper-connections, mHC, and attention or value residuals, the Lattice enhances the residual stream, but rather than adding or rerouting residual pathways through depth, it provides companion computational states that persist across tokens. Unlike hybrid Transformer-SSM architectures, whose recurrent states primarily serve as internal memory for sequence mixing, these latents directly condition the vertical stream's attention, KV-cache writes, MLP computation, and decoding. On Transformers trained on 20B FineWeb-Edu tokens, a 406M-parameter Lattice model lowers validation perplexity from 15.41 to 14.40 over a parameter-matched Transformer. These gains extend across architectural variants. The Lattice composes with prior residual-stream enhancements: with hyper-connections and input residuals, it lowers validation perplexity from 15.41 \(base Transformer\) and 14.22 \(Transformer with the same enhancements\) to 13.61. On Gated DeltaNet, Mamba-2, Triadic GDN, and GDN-Mamba-2 hybrids, it improves perplexity at every tested native state size, with Lattice-GDN and Lattice-Triadic-GDN variants outperforming pure baselines with 4x larger native states. These results identify persistent per-layer state as a complementary axis for scaling language-model computation.
  - [ViRA: Toward Ultra-High-Resolution Visual Agents via Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21605v1)
    - 摘要：Visual agents answer questions about ultra-high-resolution photos by zooming into the relevant regions. However, existing agents often answer from common sense instead of reading the evidence. Reinforcement learning \(RL\) on the final answer cannot correct this, because a guess earns the same reward as reading. We propose Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization \(CrossRPO\), which trains agents on contrast sets. A contrast set asks one question about several versions of an image that differ only in a small region with the evidence and have different answers, so only reading the evidence solves the whole set. CrossRPO rewards solving the whole set, and we prove that it does not reinforce a set answered from common sense. We build contrast sets for training \(ViRA-Train\) and evaluation \(ViRA-Bench\) by editing one small object in images of 21 to 120 megapixels, and we photograph further sets with real changes \(ViRA-Bench-Real\) to exclude editing traces. We train two Visual Reasoning Agents, ViRA-9B and ViRA-27B, with CrossRPO on only about 3,500 items of ViRA-Train each. CrossRPO achieves 63.3% set accuracy on ViRA-Bench at the maximum resolution, against 59.3% for GRPO. ViRA-9B is the most accurate open model of its size on all benchmarks, and ViRA-27B outperforms Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6-Luna, both at their highest thinking level, on most benchmarks.

  - [Ambient LLMs: Data-Efficient Supervised Fine-tuning via Temperature Matching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23229v1)
    - 摘要：Collecting large-scale, diverse, and high-quality post-training datasets for autoregressive language models is challenging. Consequently, Supervised Fine-Tuning \(SFT\) is typically applied to datasets significantly smaller than those used for pre-training, which leads to overfitting and the catastrophic forgetting of the tails of the data distribution. To prevent this collapse, we introduce Ambient SFT, a principled framework for leveraging imperfect predictions to augment the fine-tuning set. In our method, a "frozen" version of the pre-trained model serves as a noisy teacher that the fine-tuned model must match at a coarse level. In particular, we require that the high-temperature versions of the conditional distributions produced by the post-training model should match the high-temperature versions of the pre-training ones. The temperature hyperparameter controls the level of trust in the pre-trained model, with the limit $T\\to \\infty$ reverting back to regular SFT and $T=1$ to knowledge distillation. We demonstrate that our approach outperforms standard SFT across domain adaptation, instruction-tuning, and reasoning, leading for up to $21\\%$ average relative improvement to standard SFT.
  - [Spool: Low-Latency Streaming Inference for Hybrid Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23228v1)
    - 摘要：Applications such as visual accessibility agents and robotics require answering questions about recent video content promptly, over a bounded window of past frames \(e.g., the last two minutes\). Hybrid vision-language models \(VLMs\), which interleave attention with linear recurrent layers such as Gated DeltaNet \(GDN\), are attractive for this setting, but maintaining a sliding window is expensive. The reason is that recurrent layers combine all past frames into a shared state, so expiring old frames normally requires re-running the entire window through the model. We observe that the recurrent update is affine in the state with input-dependent coefficients, so the effect of each frame can be captured as a transformation that composes associatively. We present Spool, a training-free framework that caches per-frame state transformations and composes them efficiently as the window advances, removing expired frames from each recurrent layer's state without replaying frames through the backbone. Removal is exact within each layer; as with key-value eviction in attention, expired frames can retain residual influence through the cached inputs computed by earlier layers. We design a dedicated data structure and GPU kernels to achieve low caching and composition overhead. Across five open-source hybrid VLMs and four video reasoning benchmarks, Spool achieves the lowest p95 time-to-first-token in all 20 model-benchmark tests, reducing it by a geometric mean of $6.6\\times$ over the faster of two recent vLLM baselines, while improving accuracy by 2.3 percentage points on average.

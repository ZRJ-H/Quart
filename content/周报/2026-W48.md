  - [Spectrum Adaptation of AI-Native Autoencoder Communications with Transfer Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34942v1)
    - 摘要：End-to-end physical-layer learning replaces separate modulation, coding, and detection operations with an autoencoder \(AE\) that consists of a jointly trained encoder-decoder pair at the transmitter and the receiver. This paper studies AI-native communications with AEs and quantifies the sensitivity to training-test mismatch in channel models. Models trained only on an AWGN channel achieve low bit error rates \(BERs\) under matched conditions but degrade severely on Rayleigh fading with practical front-end impairments. To reduce the time and energy required to adapt to new channel conditions, we propose a low-latency transfer-learning procedure that starts from an AWGN-pretrained system and fine-tunes only a small subset of transmitter and receiver layers while freezing the remainder. We further formulate layer selection as a budget-constrained optimization problem that identifies the most informative parameters to update during adaptation. This preserves the learned signal geometry and the deep decision structure while adapting lightweight front ends to fading- and impairment-induced mixing. For AE communications, we show that partial updates remove the mismatch-induced error floor and approach the BER of fully retrained models. We provide a layer-wise study and quantify resource-performance trade-offs, showing that modest adaptation substantially reduces BER while cutting training time, multiply-accumulate \(MAC\) operations, energy consumption, and the fraction of updated parameters for resource-efficient implementation.
